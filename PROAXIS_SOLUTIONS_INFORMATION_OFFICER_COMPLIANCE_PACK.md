## PART 3 — DATA SUBJECT ACCESS REQUEST (DSAR) PROCEDURE

A data subject may submit a request to:
- Access personal information held about them;
- Correct inaccurate information;
- Request deletion or destruction of information;
- Object to processing.

### 3.1 Receiving a DSAR

All DSARs must be directed to the Information Officer. They may arrive via:
- Email to the IO email address;
- Written request to the company postal address;
- Via the company website (once a DSAR form is published).

### 3.2 DSAR Processing Procedure

| Step | Action | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acknowledge receipt of the DSAR in writing | Within 5 business days |
| 2 | Verify the identity of the requestor (request certified ID copy if needed) | Within 5 business days |
| 3 | Locate the requested information | As soon as possible |
| 4 | Assess whether any grounds for refusal apply | Before response |
| 5 | Provide the information or state reasons for refusal in writing | Within the period prescribed by POPIA [VERIFY: current period] |
| 6 | Log the request in the IO Register (Section 5 of this pack) | Immediately upon receipt |
| 7 | File all documentation related to the request | Upon closure |

### 3.3 Grounds for Refusing a DSAR

A DSAR may be refused on the grounds permitted by POPIA, including (but not limited to) [VERIFY: applicable POPIA provisions]:

- The information does not exist or is not held by Proaxis Solutions;
- Providing the information would disclose personal information of a third party;
- Providing the information would disclose legally privileged communications;
- The request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

If a DSAR is refused, the IO must provide written reasons referencing the applicable POPIA provision.

### 3.4 Data Subject Remedies

Inform the requestor that if they are dissatisfied with the outcome, they may lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator of South Africa [VERIFY: current complaint procedure — www.inforegulator.org.za].

## PART 6 — DATA BREACH RESPONSE PROCEDURE

### 6.1 Immediate Response (Within 24 Hours of Detection)

1. **Contain** — Stop the breach from continuing. Isolate affected systems, devices, or accounts.
2. **Preserve** — Do not delete evidence. Preserve logs, screenshots, and any records of what occurred.
3. **Notify IO** — The person who discovers the breach must notify the Information Officer immediately at 066 213 6607 or willsonjohnny430@gmail.com [UPDATE to privacy@proaxissolutions.co.za].

### 6.2 Assessment (Within 24–48 Hours)

The IO must determine:
- What personal information was involved (categories and sensitivity);
- How many data subjects are potentially affected;
- What the likely consequences are (identity theft risk, financial harm, reputational harm, etc.);
- Whether the breach is ongoing or has been contained.

### 6.3 Notification to the Information Regulator

The IO must notify the **Information Regulator of South Africa** as soon as reasonably practicable after becoming aware that personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person. [VERIFY: current POPIA Section 22 notification requirements, including whether there is a prescribed form and portal for notification — www.inforegulator.org.za]

**Information to include in the notification** [VERIFY: current prescribed notification content]:
- Company name and registration number;
- IO name and reference number;
- Description of the breach (what happened, when, how);
- Categories and approximate number of data subjects affected;
- Categories and approximate number of records affected;
- Likely consequences of the breach;
- Measures taken or proposed to address the breach.

### 6.4 Notification to Affected Data Subjects

Where the breach is likely to result in harm to data subjects (e.g., identity theft, financial loss, discrimination), the IO must notify the affected data subjects as soon as reasonably practicable. [VERIFY: current POPIA requirements on data subject notification — Section 22.]

The notification must describe the breach in plain language and advise data subjects on steps they can take to protect themselves.

### 6.5 Post-Breach Review

After the breach is resolved:
- Document the incident fully in the Data Breach Register;
- Review what caused the breach;
- Implement measures to prevent recurrence;
- Update security policies as required;
- Retain all documentation relating to the breach.
